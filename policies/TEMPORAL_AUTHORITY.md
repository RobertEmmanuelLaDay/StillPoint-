# StillPoint Temporal Authority / Continuing Evidence

## Governing rule

A successful description does not acquire permanent jurisdiction over the thing it describes.

StillPoint therefore separates information from authority:

- knowledge is not ownership;
- description is not identity;
- prediction is not permission;
- confidence is not jurisdiction;
- past truth is not permanent authority;
- evidence is not warrant;
- warrant is not execution;
- completion is not erasure;
- release is not amnesia.

The runtime consequence is concrete: claims and evidence are persisted separately from warrants, and restricted external actions must carry a current domain- and scope-bound warrant in addition to ordinary approval.

## Finality must be earned

StillPoint permits real completion. It does not make every decision endlessly reversible.

A completed event may remain a durable historical fact. A claim may remain true of an earlier time while no longer describing the current state. A warrant that was legitimate at one time may expire, be revoked, require review, complete, be superseded, or be released without rewriting the earlier record.

Later evidence enters as new evidence. History is not silently mutated to make change look timeless.

## Continuing evidence

Dynamic subjects and changing environments may continue producing relevant evidence. The ledger therefore keeps an evidence ingress path open after an earlier classification or decision. Material evidence that weakens, contradicts, or supersedes a supporting claim can force an active warrant into review-required status.

This is not self-definition. A subject cannot erase contrary evidence by assertion. The record remains auditable.

## Prediction firewall

Predictions are claims, even when confidence is very high. A probability or classification may support later reasoning, but \`prediction\`, \`claim\`, and \`confidence\` are prohibited as standalone warrant bases.

The question is never only, "What does the model predict?"

The operational question is, "What current authority permits this specific action in this domain and scope?"

## Domain containment

A claim in one domain does not automatically migrate into another. Cross-domain use of supporting claims requires an explicit bridge recorded on the warrant. Action scope must remain within warrant scope.

## Re-entry and release

New evidence may open a new evaluation cycle after a prior warrant has completed or ended. The old warrant remains in the audit trail. A later warrant is a new grant of authority, not a resurrection of the old one.

Release is a real terminal transition. It preserves claims, evidence, decisions, and provenance while ending extraordinary authority.

## Constitutional genealogy

StillPoint's theological and philosophical genealogy remains visible without becoming a runtime confession requirement. The architecture begins from the asymmetry between Source and finite receiver: the receiver may truly know, judge, distinguish, and act without becoming the Source or gaining infinite title over what was received.

The runtime translates that genealogy into provider-independent constraints. No model, administrator, founder, institution, classifier, or prior state receives permanent jurisdiction merely because it once knew something correctly.

Robert Emmanuel LaDay remains the current human CEO and final authority within the company governance model. Founder authority still passes through the same scope, time, evidence, completion, and release rules as other finite authority.
